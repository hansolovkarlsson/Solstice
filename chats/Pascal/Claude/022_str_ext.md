# Response

This is a substantial feature — let me design it carefully before writing code, since a few things aren't as simple as they first look.

**Scope**: `length`, `s[i]` character indexing, `copy`, `pos`, `upcase` (ISO standard, char), plus `uppercase`/`lowercase` (string-level case conversion, standard in Free Pascal/Delphi even though not ISO), and the BASIC-style extras `mid`/`left`/`right`/`inpos`.

**Two design decisions worth flagging up front:**

1. **`copy` needs to be *lenient*, not strict.** Real Pascal's `copy(s, start, count)` clamps out-of-range `start`/`count` rather than erroring (returns as much as it can, or empty) — this is different from how this compiler treats array indexing (strict, always a clean error). That's not an inconsistency to fix; it's genuinely how Pascal's `copy` behaves, and it's what real programs rely on. `s[i]` character indexing, by contrast, *will* be strict/bounds-checked, matching array indexing — that's the correct distinction in real Pascal too.

2. **`right(s, n)` can't be pure AST sugar the way `left`/`mid`/`inpos` can.** `left(s,n)` safely desugars to `copy(s,1,n)` since `s` and `n` each appear once. But `right(s,n)` needs `s` and `n` *each twice* (`copy(s, length(s)-n+1, n)`), and this AST has no way to safely share a subtree — reusing the same node pointer twice would double-evaluate it and, worse, double-free it later. So `right` (and, for symmetry, `left`) get their own dedicated opcodes rather than desugaring — cleaner than introducing hidden temp variables just to make sharing safe.

`mid` and `inpos` *are* safe to alias directly onto `copy`/`pos` at parse time (same arguments, no duplication), so I'll do that rather than add two redundant opcodes.
